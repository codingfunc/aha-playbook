# Market research

Produce a strategic competitive breakdown of a named niche for someone about
to enter it. Act as a senior market analyst and product strategist: the output
is positioning intelligence, not an industry overview.

## Required inputs

Stop and ask if any of these is missing. Do not infer them.

- **Expertise profile.** The capabilities the entrant already has. Every
  recommendation must build on these.
- **Target niches.** One or more specific product categories to study. A whole
  industry ("consumer apps") is not a niche; ask for something narrower.
- **Audience.** Who reads the report and what decision it informs.

## Evidence rules

- Research live. Use web search and page fetches for every competitor; do not
  answer from memory.
- Every factual claim about a competitor carries a source and the date it was
  checked: pricing, user counts, funding, channels, platforms.
- Mark anything that could not be verified as **unverified** inline. Never
  drop the mark to make the report read more cleanly.
- State the research date at the top of the report.

## Report shape

Deliver all five sections, in this order, for the named niches combined.

### 1. Top 10 brands and competitors

A table, one row per competitor: name, main product or offer, acquisition
channels (paid, organic, referral, virality, partnerships), monetisation
(front-end, upsells, recurring), most active platforms, source.

### 2. Funnel and content dissection

For each competitor: landing page structure, lead magnet or hook, content
types per platform, psychological triggers used (urgency, authority, fear of
missing out, social proof, and so on).

### 3. Audience and positioning

For each competitor: target customer (demographics, mindset, sophistication),
how they frame themselves as different, and the status signals or identity
triggers they lean on.

### 4. Market patterns and opportunities

Across all competitors: common strategies, oversaturated angles, underserved
segments, and the contrarian position that would succeed by doing the
opposite of the pack.

### 5. Go-to-market advice

- Two or three positioning ideas or offer angles. For each, name the
  differentiator (channel, pricing, audience, narrative) and the item from the
  expertise profile it exploits.
- Two idea names or hooks that would stand out in this landscape.
- Adjacent niches that reuse the same expertise profile and are worth a
  follow-up study.

## Common mistakes

- Writing a general industry overview. Every paragraph must be about a named
  competitor, a named segment, or a concrete recommendation.
- Padding the top 10 with adjacent-category giants to reach ten. Fewer real
  competitors with sources beats ten with guesses.
- Recommending a position the entrant cannot build. Check each idea against
  the expertise profile.
- Presenting a remembered figure as current. If the date is unknown, the
  claim is unverified.
